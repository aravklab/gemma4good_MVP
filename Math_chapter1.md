## I. 3D Shapes: Prisms and Pyramids
[cite_start]3D shapes are categorized by the nature of their faces, edges, and corners (vertices)[cite: 482].

### Core Definitions
* **Net**: A flattened, 2D representation of a 3D box. [cite_start]Folding along the dotted lines of a net recreates the solid[cite: 475].
* **Prism**: A solid with two identical ends (bases) and flat, rectangular sides. [cite_start]A cube is a special type of square prism[cite: 477, 479].
* **Pyramid**: A solid where all triangular side faces meet at a single top point (apex). [cite_start]The base determines the pyramid's name (e.g., Square Pyramid)[cite: 477].

### Properties of Solids ($F, V, E$)
A key relationship exists between the number of **Faces (F)**, **Corners/Vertices (V)**, and **Edges (E)**. [cite_start]In many cases, the relationship is expressed as $F + V - E = 2$[cite: 496, 497].

## II. Angles and Rigidity
[cite_start]Angles are formed when two lines meet at a point[cite: 615].

* [cite_start]**Right Angle**: Forms a perfect "L" shape, like the corner of a book or window[cite: 632, 637].
* [cite_start]**Acute Angle**: An angle that is "smaller" or less than a right angle[cite: 646].
* [cite_start]**Obtuse Angle**: An angle that is "larger" or more than a right angle[cite: 647].

### Shape Rigidity
* **Triangles**: The most rigid shape. [cite_start]It does not change its shape even when pushed[cite: 686].
* **Rectangles/Squares**: Not rigid. [cite_start]Pushing the side of a rectangle changes its right angles into a mix of acute and obtuse angles, forming a parallelogram or rhombus[cite: 678, 681, 682].

## III. Circle Geometry
[cite_start]Circles are unique because they have curved faces and no corners[cite: 523, 534].

* [cite_start]**Centre (A)**: The exact middle point of the circle[cite: 718, 721].
* [cite_start]**Radius**: The straight-line distance from the centre to the border[cite: 721, 740].
* [cite_start]**Diameter**: A crease or line that passes through the centre from one border to the other[cite: 736, 738].
* [cite_start]**Mathematical Rule**: The Diameter is always double the length of the Radius ($\text{Diameter} = 2 \times \text{Radius}$)[cite: 750].

## IV. Flawless Text Descriptions for Visual Puzzles
For processing by text-only LLMs, here are the detailed geometric breakdowns of the chapter's primary puzzles:

### 1. Squiggly’s Triangular Web (Page 21)
* **Description**: This is a composite 2D figure shaped like a vertical elongated hexagon or a double-ended house. It consists of a central rectangle subdivided by two diagonal lines (creating 4 internal triangles). On the top and bottom edges of this rectangle, two external triangles are attached (like a roof and a floor).
* [cite_start]**Mathematical Answer**: There are **12 individual triangles** labeled in the web [cite: 798-815].
* **The Path Challenge**: Squiggly can return to Point A without retracing steps by following the outer perimeter and then traversing the internal diagonals in a specific "envelope" pattern.

### 2. Wiggly’s Rectangular Web (Page 21)
* **Description**: A grid-like structure consisting of a large central vertical rectangle flanked by two smaller squares on the sides and one square on top.
* **Mathematical Answer**: There are multiple overlapping rectangles. Specifically, there are **7 distinct rectangular areas** (including the squares, which are special rectangles) that make up the structure.

### 3. The 5-Matchstick Triangle Challenge (Page 21)
* [cite_start]**Description**: The task asks to create 2 triangles using only 5 matchsticks[cite: 825]. 
* **Mathematical Answer**: This is achieved by creating two triangles that **share one common side**. Instead of using 3 sticks per triangle ($3 + 3 = 6$), the shared side reduces the total count to 5.

### 4. The Matchstick Transformation (Page 22)
* **Puzzle A**: A figure made of 6 matchsticks forming two adjacent triangles (like a diamond with a horizontal line through the middle).
* **Mathematical Answer**: Moving 2 matchsticks can create **4 triangles** by overlapping them or creating a smaller triangular grid within a larger one.
* **Puzzle B**: A larger triangular grid made of many matchsticks.
* **Mathematical Answer**: Removing 4 matchsticks to leave **3 triangles** requires breaking the outer perimeter so only three disconnected or minimally connected triangular units remain.

### 5. Hidden Letter Shapes (Page 19)
* **Letter A**: Constructed from 1 trapezoid and 2 triangles.
* **Letter B**: Constructed from 2 rectangles and 1 triangle.
* **Letter C**: Constructed from 1 large rectangle with a smaller rectangle "cut out" from the side.
* **Letter D**: Constructed from 1 triangle and 1 semi-circle (or 1 triangle and 1 curved face).
